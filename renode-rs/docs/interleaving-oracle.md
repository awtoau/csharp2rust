# Tier 2.5 — an oracle that can observe interleaving

Issue #52 asks for three things: contention measured at the `// SYNC(measure)`
sites, a verdict on D3, and an oracle tier that can see interleaving.

**Only the third is deliverable today, and this is it.** The runtime measurement
and final architectural verdict are recorded below as blocked, with what
specifically blocks them. The operational verdict in the meantime is explicit:
do not enact D3; preserve locks as `Mutex` and keep threading uncertified.
PLAN.md's mapping table now says the same thing.

## Why no verdict

The acceptance criterion is "once peripherals run, measure contention and hold
times at the marked sites". Peripherals do not run. Nothing in the repo
instantiates a peripheral and drives it; the only oracle is a single-threaded
replay of recorded register accesses, and #52 itself points out that a threading
difference cannot appear in that *by construction*.

Deleting 842 synchronisation points across the tree is a whole-program
decision. The count was re-derived from the current full-tree snapshot with
`python3 scripts/sync_census.py --db tmp/breadth.db`; it remains 842. Taking it
on evidence nobody can currently gather would
be the same move the project already caught once: a green tick standing in for
an observation that was never made.

| piece | what it does |

### The census

`docs/status/sync_census.json`, regenerated by `python3 scripts/sync_census.py`.

    56 lock sites, 9 distinct lock targets, 7 types

**#52 says 42 sites in the cut; the corpus says 56.** All 56 are distinct
`Lock` operations at distinct source spans in 54 methods, so this is not double
counting. The 42 is either a stale number or a different denominator, and the
gap is recorded here rather than quietly resolved in favour of whichever is
convenient. Only one of the two can be right, and the corpus is the one that
can be re-derived.

| lock target | sites | guarded fields | calls out | nested |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `NVIC.irqs` | 14 | 10 | 57 | 0 |
| `BaseClockSource.sync` | 14 | 10 | 40 | 0 |
| `GPIO.sync` | 8 | 3 | 8 | 0 |
| `STM32F1_I2C.updateLock` | 7 | 16 | 36 | 0 |
| `Logger.ActualLogger.innerLock` | 3 | 7 | 6 | 1 |
| `Logger.backendsChangeLock` | 3 | 1 | 16 | 0 |
| `STM32SPI.receiveBuffer` | 3 | 3 | 8 | 0 |
| `Logger.ActualLogger.aggregationFlushLock` | 2 | 3 | 4 | 0 |
| `NVIC.RegisterCollection` | 2 | 1 | 2 | 0 |

Three things fall out of it that reading the sites one at a time would not give
you:

- **42 guarded fields are also touched with no lock held**, after excluding
  construction-time access. `NVIC.irqs` is guarded at 14 sites and read bare in
  `SetPending`, `GetActive`, `GetPending` and `GetEnabled*`. The emitted
  `Mutex<T>` reproduces that structure faithfully, holes included, which is
  correct and worth knowing.
- **One nested lock**, in `Logger`. Lock ordering is therefore already a live
  concern in the corpus, not a hypothetical.
- **The widest critical section spans 295 operations** (`STM32F1_I2C.
  HandleDataWrite`). A static size, *not* a hold time — source cannot supply
  one.

The census excludes enum members and constants from "guarded data": 1,428 of
4,661 field accesses in the corpus are reads of things like `IRQState.Pending`,
which are immutable and unraceable. Counting them would have inflated every
lock's footprint with data no lock has ever protected.

**What the census cannot tell you, and neither can any other static artefact:
which thread enters a method.** C# does not declare it. The census localises the
DATA; D3 also needs the THREADS, and that only exists at runtime.

### The harness

`src/renode-sync` runs one experiment:

> Run the same workload with a lock and without it, over **every** interleaving,
> and compare the sets of observable outcomes.

It is deterministic. Exactly one agent runs at a time, holding a baton passed
over a channel; the scheduler decides who gets it next. Switches happen only at
declared preemption points and at lock acquisition, and exploration is a
depth-first walk over the schedule tree, so every run is reproducible from its
decision vector. There is no `sleep`, no timing, and no duration anywhere — a
concurrency test that fails one run in fifty is worse than no test.

Deleting the lock is a **parameter** (`Policy::Locked` / `Policy::Unlocked`),
never an edit to generated code, so nothing that differs between the two arms
can be attributed to the code having changed.

It reports one of four verdicts, and the first is the important one:

| verdict | meaning |
|---|---|
| `Vacuous` | no agent was ever scheduled while another was inside a critical section — agreement here means nothing was tested |
| `LoadBearing` | removing the lock produced outcomes the locked arm cannot produce |
| `Deadlock` | a schedule deadlocked, reported with the decision vector rather than hung |
| `Indistinguishable` | every unlocked outcome is one the locked arm also produces |

**`Vacuous` exists because this project has already shipped a checker that
reported success while verifying nothing.** A critical section with no
preemption point inside it is atomic to this harness whether or not it is atomic
in reality; that must not be reported as agreement, and it is not.

### What it can and cannot see

It sees **atomicity violations** — a critical section observed or mutated
part-way through: lost updates, torn invariants, a state machine caught between
two consistent states. Those are what `lock` in the corpus defends against.

It does **not** see memory-model effects: reordering, tearing, missing fences.
It cannot, because it never lets two agents run at once — which is also why it
is free of both flakiness and undefined behaviour. A model that serialises
execution cannot report a race that only a weak memory model would produce.

## The proof it can fail

`python3 scripts/check_sync_harness.py` runs the suite twice: once as it stands,
and once with `--features prove-the-harness-can-fail`, which deletes the lock
from the locked arm of every experiment. Four tests must fail by name, and the
script fails if any of them survives:

    clean run: 11 test(s), 0 failing
    mutated run (lock deleted): 11 test(s), 4 failing
      caught   tests::a_deleted_lock_is_caught
      caught   tests::locking_leaves_only_serial_outcomes
      caught   deleting_the_lock_loses_a_real_write
      caught   deleting_the_lock_tears_an_invariant_across_two_registers

The mutation is a cargo feature rather than a text edit, so nothing is patched
in place and there is no half-mutated tree to recover.

Two of the four run against `renode_regs::Bank` — the real register DSL — driven
by write values lifted from the captured `usart1` trace. Two controls sit
alongside them and must NOT report a difference: agents touching disjoint
registers (`Indistinguishable`), and a single bank access with no preemption
point inside it (`Vacuous`). A checker that flags everything is as useless as
one that flags nothing.

## Findings

### 1. Zero lock sites have been translated

56 in the corpus, **0 in emitted Rust**. Counted, not asserted — the scorecard
recomputes both numbers. The lock-bearing types are `NVIC`, `BaseClockSource`,
`GPIO`, `STM32F1_I2C`, `Logger` and `STM32SPI`, and none of them has been
converted.

So the instrument exists and has nothing yet to point at. `SyncMutex<T>` is
shaped like `std::sync::Mutex` precisely so that the emitted
`let _sync = x.lock().unwrap();` compiles against it unchanged the day one is.

### 2. A peripheral is `Send` but not `Sync`, so today a missing lock is unrepresentable

`renode-regs` says an index-based bank is `Send`, "which `Rc` blocks and which
N-instance test parallelism needs". That is true, and it is now proven by a
doctest that compiles.

But `Send` moves a whole peripheral to a worker thread. It does not let two
threads touch one peripheral, which is the case a lock exists for. The bank's
arena is `Vec<Cell<u64>>`, so the bank is **not `Sync`**, and rustc rejects the
sharing outright — proven by a `compile_fail,E0277` doctest.

That is a stronger statement than "untested". In the current architecture the
risk D3 is arguing about cannot be expressed at all: sharing a peripheral across
threads requires an unsafe assertion, which is what `renode_sync::Shared` is and
why its safety argument is written out. Whether that stays true is a design
decision nobody has taken yet.

### 3. Resolution is bounded by preemption points, and today there are none

`Bank::read` and `Bank::write` are each a single opaque call. At bank
granularity every access is atomic, so an experiment at that level reports
`Vacuous` — correctly, and that is asserted by a test rather than described.

Interleaving becomes observable one level up, where a peripheral method makes
two bank calls that must not be split. That is exactly the shape
`lock (receiveBuffer)` guards in `STM32SPI`, and the read-modify-write that
`lock (irqs)` guards in `NVIC`.

## What is still needed before D3 can honestly be decided

In dependency order. None of it is in this issue's scope.

1. **Translate a lock-bearing peripheral.** `STM32SPI` is the cheapest: 3 sites,
   one lock, 3 guarded fields. Until then the harness has no subject.
2. **Decide whether a peripheral is reachable from more than one thread.** This
   is the actual D3 question, and it is a design decision about the Rust
   emulator's architecture, not a fact to be measured about the C#. If a
   peripheral is only ever reached from one thread, every lock in it is dead
   weight regardless of what the C# does. If the IRQ path, the time framework or
   a host-side feeder reaches it, the locks are load-bearing.
3. **Give the DSL preemption points**, or accept whole-access granularity.
   Without them the harness cannot see inside a bank operation, and it will
   keep saying so.
4. **Then, and only then, measure.** Contention and hold time need a running
   emulator. The census names the sites; nothing else about them is derivable
   from source.

Until step 2 is answered, "measure contention at the marked sites" is not a
measurement anyone can take: with one thread the answer is zero contention
everywhere, and that number would be an artefact of the architecture rather than
evidence about it.

## Acceptance status

| requirement | status | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| contention and hold times from a real workload | **blocked** | no runnable machine currently instantiates and drives a translated lock-bearing peripheral |
| written D3 verdict and reconciled plan | **interim verdict complete; final verdict blocked** | locks stay pending runtime evidence; `PLAN.md` maps `lock(obj)` to `Mutex` plus `SYNC(measure)` |
| oracle observes interleaving, or scorecard says uncertified | **complete** | tier 2.5 observes atomicity violations and the generated scorecard says **Threading is UNCERTIFIED** |

Issue #52 is therefore not close-ready. The missing work is runtime integration
and measurement, not another source-only rule or a synthetic timing number.
